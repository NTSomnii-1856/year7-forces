Learning intentions

Forces - a guide that starts from the beginning.

A force can change what an object does. We will learn to identify forces, draw them and use them to explain everyday situations.

By the end, I will be able to...

  • describe a force as a push or a pull, measured in newtons;
  • name common forces and sort them into contact and non-contact forces;
  • draw force arrows with correct labels, directions and relative lengths;
  • explain the difference between mass and weight and calculate weight;
  • find a resultant force and explain balanced and unbalanced forces.

Learn

What is a force?

A force is a push or a pull caused by one object acting on another.

  • A hand can push a door. A rope can pull a sledge.
  • A force has a size and a direction.
  • A force acts on an object. Always ask: which object am I describing?

Try it

You pull a drawer open. Which object exerts the pull, and which object receives it?

Check the answer

Your hand exerts the pull. The drawer is the object receiving the force.

Learn

What can a force change?

Forces can change an object's motion or shape.

What can change?Example
Speed increasesA kick makes a stationary ball move.
Speed decreasesBrakes slow a bicycle.
Direction changesA racket sends a ball in a new direction.
Shape changesA hand stretches an elastic band.

A force does not always make an object move. Other forces may balance it. We will compare forces later in the guide.

Try it

A ball moves at the same speed but changes direction. Has its motion changed?

Check the answer

Yes. Motion includes direction as well as speed.

Measuring a force

Force is measured in newtons. The unit symbol is N.

  1. Check that the unloaded meter reads zero.
  2. Apply the force to the hook.
  3. Read the scale beside the pointer and write the unit N.

Try it

The pointer in the diagram is beside 3. What is the force?

Check the answer

3 N. A number without its unit is not a complete measurement.

Contact or non-contact?

Contact means the objects are touching. Non-contact forces act without touching.

Contact forcesNon-contact forces
FrictionGravitational force (including weight)
Support from a surfaceMagnetic force
Tension in a ropeElectrostatic force
Air and water resistance 
Elastic force and upthrust 

Air resistance is a contact force. Moving objects collide with air particles, even though we cannot see the particles.

A magnet can attract an iron object across a gap. A charged balloon can attract small pieces of paper without first touching them.

Try it

Is Earth pulling a falling ball a contact force?

Check the answer

No. Gravity acts without contact. It is a non-contact force.

Magnetic and electrostatic forces

Both can act across a gap: they are non-contact forces.

  • Magnetic force: two magnets can attract or repel. A magnet also attracts some materials, such as iron.
  • Electrostatic force: electrically charged objects can attract or repel. Rubbing a balloon can give it an electric charge.

Try it

A balloon attracts paper without touching it. Is this a contact force?

Check the answer

No. The electrostatic force acts across the gap.

How to draw a force

A force arrow shows both size and direction.

  1. Choose the object and sketch it simply.
  2. Start each force arrow on the object.
  3. Point the arrowhead in the direction the force acts.
  4. Label the force. If its size is known, include the number and N.
  5. Use equal lengths for equal forces and a longer arrow for a larger force.

In this guide, any grey dashed arrow is labelled movement. It is not a force arrow.

Try it

Two opposite forces are both 5 N. How should their arrow lengths compare?

Check the answer

The two arrows must be equal in length.

Learn

Draw a book on a desk

Worked example: the book is at rest on a horizontal desk.

The book does not speed up or move vertically. Support and weight are equal in size, so the two arrows have equal lengths.

Try it

Does the book have no forces on it because it is not moving?

Check the answer

No. Weight and support both act. Their effects balance.

What is mass?

Mass tells us how much matter is in an object.

  • The usual unit of mass is the kilogram (kg). Smaller masses may be given in grams (g).
  • Use a balance to measure mass.
  • The mass of the same object stays the same when it is taken to another world.

Convert grams before calculating weight

1 kg = 1000 g

To change grams into kilograms, divide by 1000.

200 g = 0.20 kg

Try it

Which has more mass: 400 g or 0.4 kg?

Check the answer

They have the same mass: 400 divided by 1000 is 0.4 kg.

Learn

What is weight?

Weight is the force of gravity acting on an object.

  • Weight is a force, so its unit is the newton (N).
  • Near Earth, weight acts downwards, towards Earth.
  • A larger mass has a larger weight in the same gravitational field.
weight = mass × gravitational field strength
W = m × g
SymbolMeaningUnit
WWeightN
mMasskg
gGravitational field strengthN/kg

Worked example: A mass of 0.20 kg on Earth has weight 0.20 × 10 = 2 N.

Try it

A bag has mass 3 kg. Find its weight on Earth using g = 10 N/kg.

Check the answer

W = 3 × 10 = 30 N. The mass is still 3 kg.

Same mass, different weight

Gravitational field strength tells us the weight of each kilogram.

A 1 kg object has weight 10 N on Earth and 1.6 N on the Moon. Its mass is 1 kg in both places.

MassWeight
Measured in kgMeasured in N
Amount of matterGravitational force
Same object: same massChanges when g changes

Try it

A 2 kg object is taken to the Moon. Does its mass become smaller?

Check the answer

No. Its mass stays 2 kg. Its weight becomes 2 × 1.6 = 3.2 N.

Friction and grip

Friction acts between surfaces that touch. It opposes sliding or attempted sliding.

Rough surfaces usually produce more friction than smooth surfaces because the surfaces catch on one another more.

  • Friction is useful for grip and braking.
  • Friction can slow a sliding object and cause surfaces to warm up.
  • Lubrication can reduce friction between moving surfaces.

Try it

A box is already sliding left across a floor. In which direction does friction on the box act?

Check the answer

Right. It opposes the sliding of the box relative to the floor.

Learn

Other contact forces

Name the force that explains the push or pull.

Water resistance is drag from water. Like air resistance, it opposes motion through the fluid.

Try it

A lamp hangs from a cable. What is the upward force on the lamp?

Check the answer

Tension from the cable. The cable pulls the lamp upwards.

Learn

Air resistance

Air resistance is a contact force that opposes motion through the air.

Compare the same piece of paper, flat and crumpled, at the same speed.

  • At the same speed, a larger area facing the air usually gives more air resistance.
  • For the same object and shape, greater speed gives more air resistance.
  • A streamlined shape reduces drag.

Flattening or crumpling the same paper changes its shape and exposed area. It does not change its mass or weight.

Try it

Why does crumpled paper usually fall faster than the same paper left flat?

Check the answer

It presents a smaller area to the air and experiences less air resistance at the same speed. Its weight has not increased.

Learn

Resultant force

The resultant is the single force with the same overall effect as the forces acting together.

Same direction: add

6 N + 2 N = 8 N right

Opposite directions: subtract

9 N − 4 N = 5 N right

The resultant points in the direction of the larger force. These diagrams show horizontal forces only.

Try it

A pull of 7 N left opposes a pull of 3 N right. Find the resultant.

Check the answer

7 − 3 = 4 N left.

Learn

Balanced forces

Balanced forces have a resultant of 0 N.

If the object is...Balanced forces mean...
At restIt stays at rest.
Already movingIt continues at constant speed in a straight line.

Balanced forces do not mean that no forces act. They mean the forces have no overall effect on the motion.

Try it

A car travels at constant speed in a straight line. The driving force is 500 N forward. What is the total resistance?

Check the answer

500 N backwards. The forces balance, so the resultant is 0 N.

Learn

Unbalanced forces

Unbalanced forces have a resultant that is not zero. The motion changes.

Resultant compared with movementWhat happens?
In the direction of movementThe object speeds up.
Opposite the movementThe object slows down.
Sideways to the movementThe direction of movement changes.

A resultant force does not have to point in the direction an object is already moving.

Try it

A bicycle is moving forward and braking. Does it instantly start moving backwards?

Check the answer

No. The backwards resultant first makes its forward speed decrease.

Learn

A falling object

Weight acts downwards. Air resistance acts upwards while the object falls.

As speed increases, air resistance increases. When the forces balance, the falling speed is called terminal velocity.

Try it

At terminal velocity, is the object stationary?

Check the answer

No. It is still falling. Its speed is constant because the resultant is 0 N.

Going further

What changes when a parachute opens?

Going further: consider the parachutist and parachute together.

Opening the parachute increases the area facing the air. The total mass and weight stay the same in this example.

Try it

Does an upward resultant mean the parachutist must already be moving upwards?

Check the answer

No. While moving down, an upward resultant makes the parachutist slow down.
